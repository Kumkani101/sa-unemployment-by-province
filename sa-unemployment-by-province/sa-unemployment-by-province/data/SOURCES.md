# Data sources

All figures are the **official unemployment rate** by province for the second quarter (April to June) of each year, taken from Statistics South Africa's *Quarterly Labour Force Survey* (QLFS) statistical release P0211. Each release reports the current quarter and the same quarter one year earlier, so four releases cover eight years.

| `source_release` code | Years covered | Release and table |
|---|---|---|
| Q2_2020 | 2019, 2020 | QLFS Quarter 2: 2020, Table F (Unemployment rate by province). https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02112ndQuarter2020.pdf |
| Q2_2022 | 2021, 2022 | QLFS Quarter 2: 2022, Table E (Unemployment rate by province). https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02112ndQuarter2022.pdf |
| Q2_2024 | 2023, 2024 | QLFS Quarter 2: 2024, Table E (Unemployment rate by province). https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02112ndQuarter2024.pdf |
| Q2_2026 | 2025, 2026 | QLFS Quarter 2: 2026, Table E (Unemployment rate by province). https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02112ndQuarter2026.pdf |

## Notes

- **Q2 2020 was collected differently.** Stats SA switched to telephone interviews during the COVID-19 lockdown, and households without a telephone were not surveyed. The release explains that many people moved out of the labour force, partly because lockdown restrictions stopped them from searching for work. The expanded unemployment rate for that quarter was 42.0%.
- **Figures are as first published** in each release. Stats SA can revise earlier estimates in later releases.
- **Mpumalanga, Q2 2026:** the QLFS release gives 36.6%. Stats SA's *Mbalo Brief* (August 2026) shows 36.3%. This project uses the figure from the statistical release.
